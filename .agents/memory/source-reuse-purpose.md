---
name: Source reuse depends on purpose, not visibility
description: Noncommercial licences cannot be treated as permission merely because a parenting-data prototype is private or offline.
---

Do not equate private/offline validation with noncommercial use. Keep a
noncommercial-only source on a purpose-specific rights hold unless its terms
or separate permission cover the actual preparation and product-development use.

**Why:** A source review can correctly find an NC licence yet incorrectly
conclude that an unpublished temporary database is licensed. Visibility and
commercial purpose are different questions; source preparation may serve a
future product.

**How to apply:** Prefer sources with clearly applicable reuse conditions for
the current scope. Retain the bibliographic and rights-review record of held
sources, but exclude their body from ingestion/search/display validation until
the relevant permission is established. Keep acquisition, reading, copying,
indexing, translation, display and external AI disclosure separate.

Retain previously verified provider/settings facts at their recorded scope;
review new source-specific permissions as a delta, not a global reset.

**Why:** New source copyright restrictions neither invalidate past technical
observations nor authorize a new transmission. Both mistakes lose the distinction
between historical evidence, source permission and current execution authority.

**How to apply:** Link dated prior records, preserve unresolved source-specific
holds, and require the current run's authorization separately. Do not inspect
credentials or call providers merely to repeat an unrelated prior check.

A never-sent flag is execution history, not evidence that a licence prohibits
AI processing. A historical operational hold also needs its recorded scope and
date; do not turn it into a blanket copyright prohibition.

**Why:** A new offline trial plan must separate reusable provider facts,
source-specific unresolved conditions, and current send authorization. Conflating
them can incorrectly reset past checks or imply permission that was never given.

**How to apply:** Keep those three axes explicit. Block transmission until the
applicable conditions and new authorization are satisfied, while stating the
actual reason for each block rather than inventing a legal restriction.

## Original provenance is not always adaptation attribution

Do not assume that showing a publisher beside an adaptation is permitted merely
because the original and translation are in separate panels.

**Why:** NHS reuse terms distinguish exact originals from adaptations and
restrict attributing the adapted wording to NHS. A disclaimer alone does not
remove a publisher/URL citation attached to the adaptation.

**How to apply:** Check the source's exact display conditions. Where adaptation
attribution is restricted, retain source/version/section and original provenance
in a separate internal audit record; render the editorial identity, required
generic licence notice and scope in the parent-facing adaptation. Preserve
ordinary source attribution for sources whose terms allow or require it.
